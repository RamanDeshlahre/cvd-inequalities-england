# Press summary and media

The role includes representing the team in media settings, including radio.
These two pieces show you can do that.

---

## 1. Press summary (max 150 words)

**Structure:** headline - one-sentence finding - key number - human meaning - one caveat - where to find more.

> **Headline:** People in England's poorest areas twice as likely to die early from heart disease *[use your real ratio]*
>
> New analysis of NHS and government data shows people living in the most deprived parts of England are [X] times more likely to die from heart and circulatory disease before 75 than those in the least deprived areas - and the gap [has not narrowed] since [year].
>
> The analysis also estimates that [N] people may have undiagnosed high blood pressure, with the biggest gaps in [more deprived areas].
>
> "[One quotable sentence in your own words - what this means for people.]"
>
> The findings describe areas rather than individuals and show links rather than causes. Full report, methods and code: [link].

---

## 2. Radio script (60 seconds, about 150 spoken words)

**Rules:** one headline number only. No percentages with decimals. No jargon. End with something a listener can do.

> *Presenter: What did you find?*
>
> "The headline is that where you live in England makes a big difference to your chances of dying early from heart and circulatory disease. In the most deprived areas, early deaths are around [twice] as common as in the most affluent areas - and that gap hasn't closed in [ten] years.
>
> One reason may be high blood pressure. It usually has no symptoms, so many people don't know they have it. We estimate around [N] people could be living with it undiagnosed, and the gaps are biggest in poorer areas.
>
> The good news is that high blood pressure is easy to check and can be treated. So my message is simple: know your numbers. If you're over 40, get your blood pressure checked - at your GP, a pharmacy, or an NHS Health Check."

---

## 3. Hard questions to practise

- "Are you saying poverty causes heart disease?" - *Answer: associations, deprivation linked to many risk factors, can't separate them with area data.*
- "How sure are you about that undiagnosed number?" - *Answer: modelled estimate, give it as "around", explain why it's still useful.*
- "Isn't this just about people's lifestyle choices?" - *Answer: risk factors are shaped by circumstances; detection and treatment gaps are about services too.*
- "Which area is worst?" - *Answer carefully: name only areas clearly outside funnel limits, explain uncertainty for small areas.*
