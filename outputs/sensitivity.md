# Sensitivity and diagnostic checks

## 1. Deprivation measure (IMD 2025 vs IMD 2019)

- Spearman correlation between the two scores: 0.978
- Areas changing quintile: 26 of 153; largest move: 1 quintile
- Under-75 CVD mortality, most vs least deprived quintile: IMD 2025 ratio 1.79 (gap 43.5); IMD 2019 ratio 1.82 (gap 44.7)
- Moved into the most deprived quintile under IMD 2025: Haringey, Newham, Tower Hamlets
- **Conclusion:** the choice of IMD version does not change the findings.

## 2. Priority-area threshold (worst 20% vs worst 25%)

- 20%: 11 areas; 25%: 16 areas
- All 20% areas still flagged at 25%: True
- Added at 25%: Kirklees, North East Lincolnshire, Peterborough, Walsall, Wigan
- **Conclusion:** the core list is stable; the threshold only changes how long it is.

## 3. What drives the modelled undiagnosed hypertension estimate?

- Spearman with recorded (crude, all-age) hypertension prevalence: 0.92
- Spearman with deprivation: -0.30
- Recorded prevalence vs deprivation: -0.03
- Lowest areas: Tower Hamlets, Manchester, Nottingham, Hackney, Newham
- Highest areas: Dorset, Isle of Wight, East Riding of Yorkshire, Northumberland, Torbay
- **Interpretation:** the estimate moves almost one-for-one with overall (crude) prevalence, which is most likely driven by age structure: lowest in young inner-city areas, highest in older coastal and rural areas. It is therefore not used as a 'detection' domain for priority areas. GP-record data (CVDPREVENT) show unconfirmed high readings are more common in the most deprived quintile.
