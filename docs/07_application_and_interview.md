## 3. Interview questions to prepare for

**About the project**
1. Walk me through your project in two minutes. *(Question, data, one method, headline, one limitation, what you'd do next.)*
2. Why did you choose a funnel plot rather than a league table?
3. What is the SII and why use it instead of just Q5 minus Q1?
4. What's the biggest limitation of your analysis?
5. What surprised you in the data?
6. If BHF published this, what would you change first?

**About the role**
7. How would you run statistics sign-off for a press release with a tight deadline?
8. A senior colleague wants to say "poverty causes heart disease" in a campaign. What do you do?
9. A journalist asks you for the worst area in England. How do you answer?
10. How would you make sure your team's code could be run by someone else if you were off sick?
11. How would you support a junior analyst who keeps making the same data errors?
12. Which BHF statistics or reports have you read, and what would you add to them?

**Practise answers out loud using STAR (Situation, Task, Action, Result), using real examples only.**

## 4. Being upfront about how you built it

BHF supports responsible use of AI in applications, as long as the work reflects your own skills. If AI tools helped scaffold your code or templates, say so briefly in the README. What matters is that you ran the analysis, made the judgement calls, wrote the findings, and can explain every line. That is also what the interview will test.
